### Bad Breaks, Better Lessons
#### By Jayden Junhang Wu
##### 5-9-2026

Bad Breaks, Better Lessons
I have had a lot of troubles when playing in tournaments. It is not supposed to be that way. Tournament golf is way harder than practice golf because tournament golf is all serious and you want to hit the least amount of shots than anyone else. Practice golf is just chill and nobody is really going for the pin. The lack of pressure in practice golf makes you just try to hit shots well and make accurate contact instead of going for the green or the pin. While all three incidents were different, it has taught me that you can’t prepare for everything.

The first one of many was the one that happened at Dennis Highlands. Although I started out with a bogey 5 on the first hole, on the second hole, things started out just the way I wanted. I hit a perfect driver and a 170 3-wood, and I had 100 in, and I hit my pitching wedge thin. I suddenly had a vision of a ball in the lip of a bunker. I didn’t really know why that thought came out, but I found out a minute later that my vision was reality. I knew which bunker I was in, but I just didn’t know where it was. I thought back to my vision and I knew where the ball was. I looked in a random hole in the bunker lip and there my ball was! I asked the officials and they told me to take an unplayable or try to get it out. Of course, I took the unplayable. The ball was inches into the lip and I could barely touch it with my hands! If I tried to hit it, it would only go deeper into the lip or I would not even hit it at all. I learned that you don’t have to take the risk for everything and sometimes you can take an unplayable and not risk injury.

The second incident was far more embarrassing than the first one. Everything that could go wrong, went wrong. I was playing in the US Kids Holiday Classic at PGA National. I started on Hole 10, and I shot a 37 on the back nine and troubles began immediately on the front nine. I hit my driver way right, hit my 3-wood under the trees and in the rough, and then I hit my nine iron pin high. I then putt my ball to about a foot for par. In my mind I'm like: “No bogey? No problem. I’ll just step over his line and tap it in.” And I accidentally tapped my ball and I got a bogey on the second easiest hole on the Fazio course. That taught me that you should always step over their line, get comfortable, and then tap it in.

The third incident unfolded all the way through the third round. I played a course on the 9th of May for a US kids local tour at Dennis Highlands. I had recently become more fit and strong and my club speed had taken a spurt. My driver shaft was flex R2, and I played a course for the Concord local tour a week ago and all my drives went to the right fairway. I thought this was no problem, since my elbow was hurting and I thought it was all about the elbow. Turns out I was wrong. I hit almost every single drive to the other hole’s rough and 3 on the other fairway. I only hit 2 fairways and the only shot I hit that wasn’t right was a pulled drive that went to the left trees. I changed my swing to hit a fade and aimed to the left. That was a huge mistake. I swung the club and aimed to fade. What I wanted to do: Tiny fade to the middle of the fairway. What actually happened: Started on the right rough and blasted to the right and ended up on the right side of the other fairway on the right. So the rest of the day I aimed at the left side of the fairway and tried to hit draws. I have only hit the right side of the rough, not the other fairway. On May 10th, mother’s day, I have withdrawn from Easton golf club for local tours and I will go to the range and try out my brother’s old shaft, which is a R standard shaft, designed for players with 85-95 Mph swing speed. I got the point that you don’t always try to hit draws when your driver’s going right and sometimes just give it a straight shot and try to get it on the fairway.

Golf taught me that it will always give you back the shots it takes from you, no matter what day it is, and no matter how bad the weather or how bad you’re playing. I wouldn’t like to have experienced all these incidents, but having these troubles just makes me more experienced. In tournaments while facing and solving these problems, it makes me more modest and calm. I have become more mature than when I was 11. While sometimes golf is stressful, it keeps you thinking from start to finish.
