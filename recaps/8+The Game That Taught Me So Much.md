## The Game That Taught Me So Much
### Jayden Jiang
#### 11-18-2025
When I first touched a golf club, I didn’t know what to do. I was confused and put a ball on the mat. My coach taught me what golf is, the rules of golf, and how to make a golf swing. These basic fundamentals that pros still practice every day. My swing, grip, ball contact, stance, everything needed to hit one ball. I practiced this every weekend when I had time. I go to the range, to the putting green, to the course. This is part of what golf is all about. A mental game that makes you want to quit when you hit a bad shot. But it teaches you how to control your anger, find ways to calm yourself down, and continue to the next hole. It also teaches you many life lessons and skills. Social skills, telling the truth, responsibility, respect, sportsmanship, patience, focus, and resilience. These are skills that can apply to anyone’s daily life.

On the course, you have to have etiquette which is course manners. This is important because destroying the course and taking huge divots out of the ground without repairing them is really bad for future golfers that go to that course. Also, having course etiquette means to respect other people while they are hitting. For example, getting your shadow out of the way, not stepping on their line when putting, keeping quiet during the backswing, and staying out of their view. These are key tips that beginners aren't aware of. My coach taught me all of these tips before I even had my own set of clubs.

In practice, I work on new techniques that would improve my swing. I used to have a problem with my head moving too much during the swing, but after a few weeks of practicing my head now barely moves. There is no such thing as the “perfect” swing or the “best” swing. The “best” swing you can do is a swing that feels comfortable and perfect for you. You don’ t needs the same swing as Tiger Woods or Rory McIlroy, you just need a swing that fits you and you can hit the ball solidly with. But grip is important too. If you made a homemade grip, then it is probably unreliable and doesn't steady the club. Most players use a grip that is universally used like the overlap or the interlock. I use the overlap, and I like it because it feels comfortable.

Most weekends, I have US kids tournaments. In these events, I am an inconsistent player and can sometimes shoot well and sometimes shoot horribly. However, my experience in tournaments is now improving because I made new friends, so I feel comfortable around them. Now, I play all the tournaments I can to gain more experience and remember the course for next time. I started golfing not so long ago, so I am one of the weaker players and still trying to improve on my score and practice more. But I can still get first place and beat other better players. It just takes a lot of courage and a confident mentality to do it.

To conclude, I fell in love with golf because it pushed me to my limits and it also made me a more mature and better version of myself. Golf helps with mental and physical health and game, it helps with breathing and calming yourself down and keeping you healthy.
