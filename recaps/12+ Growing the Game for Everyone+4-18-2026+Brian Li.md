## Growing the Game for Everyone - A Reflection on the Winter Adaptive Golf Program 
### By Brian Li, President of the Boston Eaglets Club
#### 4-18-2026

In early January 2026, the Boston Eaglets Club launched its first six-week Winter Indoor Adaptive Golf Program for children ages 6–12 from AllStars Learning, a nonprofit organization serving children with special needs. Our goal was simple: create an opportunity for these children to stay active during the cold Boston winter while experiencing the game of golf.

Because winters in Boston are long and snowy, outdoor sports can be difficult. To make the program possible, we transformed the indoor space at my house into a small training facility with three putting stations, two indoor chipping areas using soft golf balls, and a simulator room equipped with a computer and projector. Each weekend, four students joined us and were paired with Boston Eaglets Club members serving as junior coaches.

Some students were shy at first and barely spoke, but after a few weeks many began to open up—talking with their junior coaches, laughing with others, and showing more confidence with every visit. Every child learns at their own pace: some could stay focused longer, while others need different teaching approaches. Our club members quickly adapted, sometimes working in pairs to support one student and finding creative ways to keep the activity engaging and fun. We were also pleasantly surprised to discover that two students showed remarkable natural talent, quickly understanding instructions and chipping many shots into the target area.

After just two weeks, many other families learned about the program, and parents asked if more children could participate. Our original four practice stations soon became crowded, so we reorganized the space and expanded to six and even seven stations running at the same time. By the end of the six-week winter program, twelve students had participated, and many expressed strong interest in continuing when spring arrived. Encouraged by families and AllStars Learning, our club members decided to continue the program outdoors despite our own busy golf schedules in the spring.
Today we are running the second six-week Outdoor Adaptive Golf Program, where students can practice more aspects of the game, including iron shots and longer swings. As the program grows, however, equipment has become a challenge. The junior clubs we collected are no longer enough for the increasing number of students. To help solve this problem, we launched a community equipment donation effort, inviting families to donate gently used junior golf clubs so more children can participate.

Looking ahead, Boston Eaglets Club hopes to continue expanding the Adaptive Golf Program and making the game more accessible to every child who wants to play. This summer, we plan to organize a charity golf tournament to raise funds and bring even more children into the program, continuing our mission of growing the game for everyone. Families who would like to support the program or donate gently used junior golf clubs are welcome to connect with us through our website.
